Keeping your tires properly inflated is one of the simplest, most cost-effective ways to improve fuel economy, extend tire life, and stay safe on the road. Yet many drivers rely on the inaccurate gauges at gas stations or skip checking altogether. A good budget gauge, one that reads consistently and feels comfortable in your hand, costs little and pays for itself many times over. When choosing a budget gauge, focus on three things: accuracy, ease of use, and durability. Analog gauges with a dial or pencil design are often cheaper and need no batteries, but they can be harder to read in low light. Digital gauges offer precise readings and backlit screens, but they may require battery changes. Look for a gauge with a sturdy housing, a flexible hose or angled head for reaching awkward valve stems, and a pressure range that covers your vehicle’s needs, most cars run between 30 and 35 PSI, but trucks and SUVs may need more.
